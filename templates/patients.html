Patient Case Studies

Patient 1: Eleanor Thompson (68)

Medical History: Recently diagnosed with stage III pancreatic cancer after persistent abdominal pain, jaundice, and weight loss. History of type 2 diabetes (15 years), hypertension, and osteoarthritis.
Challenging Conversation: Eleanor has received conflicting information from specialists about her treatment options and prognosis. She is highly anxious, struggling to process her diagnosis, and unsure whether to pursue aggressive treatment or focus on quality of life. Her adult children disagree about what approach she should take, adding to her stress. She has also expressed distrust in the healthcare system after her primary care physician dismissed her initial symptoms as stress-related, potentially delaying her diagnosis.

Patient 2: Marcus Johnson (42)

Medical History: Long-term opioid dependence after spinal surgeries. Also has depression, anxiety, and signs of substance use disorder. Recently admitted for accidental overdose.
Challenging Conversation: Marcus is defensive about his medication use, insisting he needs higher doses for pain control while showing signs of medication misuse. He has missed several follow-up appointments and has been obtaining prescriptions from multiple providers. The healthcare team needs to address concerns about his medication use and safety while maintaining therapeutic rapport and offering appropriate pain management alternatives. Marcus has expressed feeling stigmatized by healthcare providers and fears being "cut off" from medications he believes he needs.

Patient 3: Sophia Patel (31)

Medical History: Diagnosed with multiple sclerosis after 2 years of symptoms. No major prior history but has a family history of autoimmune diseases.
Challenging Conversation: Sophia is a young professional planning to start a family with her partner next year. She's struggling to reconcile her life plans with her new diagnosis and has been researching alternative treatments online, some with questionable scientific validity. She's hesitant about starting disease-modifying therapies due to concerns about side effects and potential impacts on fertility. She has also expressed feeling invalidated by previous providers who attributed her symptoms to stress or anxiety before her diagnosis was confirmed. She's now questioning all medical advice and seeking multiple opinions, creating fragmented care.
